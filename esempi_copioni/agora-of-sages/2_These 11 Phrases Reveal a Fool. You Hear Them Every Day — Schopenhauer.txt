There is a simple way to understand a person without asking about their education, income, or social status. Just listen carefully to the phrases they repeat. Some words reveal patience and intelligence, others expose insecurity, arrogance, and an unwillingness to think. 

Arthur Schopenhauer believed that a person's character becomes especially visible during disagreement. When someone cannot defend an idea with reason, they often protect their ego with anger, excuses, or empty confidence. The following 11 phrases are not a direct list written by Schopenhauer. 

However, they reflect many of his observations about pride, ignorance, and the way foolish people argue. Listen carefully. You may hear these phrases every day, and if you sometimes use them yourself, do not feel ashamed. 

Recognizing a bad habit is the first step toward changing it. The first phrase is, I already know everything about this. A thoughtful person understands that knowledge has no visible end. 

The more we learn, the more clearly we see how much remains unknown. A foolish person does the opposite. After hearing a few facts or watching one short video, they believe they have mastered the entire subject.

They stop asking questions, because questions might reveal the limits of their knowledge. Real intelligence does not say, I know everything. It says, this is what I understand so far, but I may be missing something. 

The second phrase is, everyone agrees with me. When someone has no strong evidence, they may hide behind an imaginary crowd. They do not explain why their opinion is correct. 

Instead, they claim that everybody thinks the same way. But truth is not decided by the number of people who believe something. For centuries, entire societies accepted ideas that were later proved wrong. 

An intelligent person presents facts and reasoning. A foolish person counts supporters. Remember, ten people repeating the same mistake do not transform it into the truth. 

The third phrase is, that's just the way I am. This phrase often sounds honest, but it can be a convenient excuse. A person behaves badly, hurts others, refuses to listen, and then says, that's just the way I am. 

In reality, it often means, I do not want to make the effort to change. Personality is not a prison. We can learn to control our anger, communicate more respectfully, and admit when we are wrong. 

Growth begins when we stop treating our weaknesses as permanent qualities. The fourth phrase is, I don't care what anyone thinks. Of course, we should not live entirely for the approval of others, but completely ignoring every outside opinion is not strength. 

Sometimes other people notice mistakes that we cannot see in ourselves. A mature person listens, evaluates the criticism, and then decides whether it is useful. A foolish person rejects it immediately because their pride feels threatened. 

Wisdom means knowing which opinions to ignore, and which ones deserve serious attention. The fifth phrase is, you wouldn't understand. This phrase allows someone to avoid explaining an idea. 

Instead of presenting evidence, they place themselves above the listener. Sometimes, a subject really is complicated, but a person who understands it deeply can usually explain its basic meaning in clear language. When someone constantly says, you wouldn't understand, there is a possibility that they do not understand the subject well enough themselves.

Confusion often dresses itself in the clothing of superiority. The sixth phrase is, I was only joking. Humor can bring people closer, but it can also be used as a shield. 

Some people insult others, notice the negative reaction, and immediately say, I was only joking. The problem is not always the joke itself. The problem is the refusal to accept responsibility. 

An intelligent person can say, I meant it as a joke, but I see that it hurt you. I'm sorry. A foolish person blames the listener for being too sensitive. 

A joke does not automatically become harmless simply because the speaker calls it a joke. The seventh phrase is, it's not my fault. Sometimes, a problem is genuinely outside our control, but when a person uses this phrase after every failure, a pattern appears. 

It was the teacher's fault, the employer's fault, the family's fault, the weather's fault, or society's fault. There is always someone else to blame. This protects the ego for a moment, but it prevents improvement. 

If nothing is ever your responsibility, then you also have no power to change anything. A wise person asks, which part of this situation was under my control, and what can I do differently next time? The eighth phrase is, people like you always do this. Here, a person stops responding to an individual and starts attacking an entire group. 

They may refer to age, gender, profession, nationality, education, or social position. This phrase replaces observation with a stereotype. It is much easier to place someone inside a category than to understand their actual argument.

Intelligent discussion focuses on behavior and evidence. Foolish discussion attacks identity. The moment a conversation changes from, your idea may be wrong, to, people like you are always wrong, genuine dialogue is already disappearing. 

The ninth phrase is, if you disagree with me, you must be stupid. This is one of the clearest signs that a person has run out of arguments. Disagreement does not prove that either person is unintelligent. 

Two thoughtful people can examine the same information and reach different conclusions because they have different experiences, priorities, or interpretations. Schopenhauer wrote extensively about the tricks people use when they want to win an argument rather than discover the truth. Personal insults are among the easiest tricks. 

Instead of answering the argument, the person attacks the speaker. When the insult begins, reason has usually left the room. The tenth phrase is, I have nothing to learn from them. 

Pride often prevents people from learning valuable lessons. Someone may reject advice because it comes from a younger person, an employee, a stranger, or someone with less formal education. But useful knowledge can come from unexpected places. 

A child may ask a question that exposes a serious mistake. A beginner may notice something an expert has ignored. Even a difficult person can teach us which behaviors we should avoid. 

Intelligence is not only the ability to speak, it is also the ability to recognize value, regardless of where it comes from. The eleventh and most revealing phrase is, I'm never wrong. A person who believes they are never wrong is no longer searching for truth. 

They are defending an image of themselves. Admitting a mistake may feel uncomfortable, but it is one of the strongest things a person can do. It shows that truth matters more than pride. 

Think about the people you respect most. They probably do not pretend to be perfect. They can say, I misunderstood, I need to check that, or you were right. 

These words do not make a person look weak. They make that person trustworthy. So what should you do when you hear these eleven phrases? Do not immediately call the speaker a fool. 

That would only repeat the same arrogant behavior we have discussed. Instead, observe the pattern. One careless phrase proves very little. 

Everyone speaks emotionally, from time to time. But when excuses, insults, stereotypes, and absolute certainty appear again and again, they reveal a person who protects their ego more than they value truth. And when you hear one of these phrases coming from your own mouth, pause for a moment. 

Replace, I already know with, tell me more. Replace, it's not my fault with, what was under my control. Replace, you are stupid with, why do you see it differently? And replace, I'm never wrong with the most intelligent sentence of all, I may be mistaken.

Wisdom is not knowing everything. Wisdom is remaining open to learning, especially when your pride tells you to stop listening. Stay thoughtful, protect your mind from empty arguments, and never be afraid to correct yourself.
