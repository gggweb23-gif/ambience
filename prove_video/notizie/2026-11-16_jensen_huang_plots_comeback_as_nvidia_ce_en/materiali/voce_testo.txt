eleven_v3|pNInz6obpgDQGcFmaJgB|Jensen Huang is reportedly trying to get his job back. Days after he resigned from Nvidia, allies are said to be running a war room to lobby the board. The Financial Times reported Sunday that Huang has discussed a comeback with confidants. It cited people familiar with the matter. One ally told the paper they were assembling a war room of Silicon Valley executives. The plan: lobby the board, and seek support from the White House. Quote from that ally, as reported by the FT: everyone that matters in tech outside the frontier labs loves Jensen. The person added that the effort has Huang's support. The Wall Street Journal says allies are working through open letters, phone calls and group chats. Their argument: the ouster was hasty. And then there is the petition. It is called Stand With Jensen, and it crossed 175,000 signatures by Monday morning, according to the Journal. But that's not the whole story. How did one of the most powerful CEOs on earth end up here, in just a few days? On November 12, Nvidia announced that Huang had resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately. He had led the company he co-founded for 33 years. It followed reports that he had affairs, one with a woman U.S. intelligence deemed a Chinese spy, according to Fortune. Huang had earlier apologized and said there was no excuse. Here is the other side, in the board's own words. On November 8, it said it had no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised. An independent investigation, it said, would examine all available information. Then the board changed course. It accepted his resignation and named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO, while it searches for a permanent chief. Harvey Jones became Nvidia's first-ever board chair. Before that appointment, according to Fortune, the board operated without a chair, and the company had no clear succession plan. The market reacted fast. CNN reported that Nvidia is down about 30.5% from its October 6 peak, a loss of roughly $1.76 trillion in market value. Now the campaign. Elon Musk said on X that he signed the petition. According to CNBC, he posted it on Friday. On Saturday, Musk went further. He wrote that many of Huang's supplier deals were, quote, a handshake with Jensen, not a written contract. Without Jensen, he said, some critical deals will be lost. The other side: Nvidia says operations, customer commitments and its roadmap will continue without interruption. And no specific agreement has been confirmed lost. Who else has signed? According to the Journal, confirmed signers include Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick, Shopify CEO Tobias Lütke and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney. Y Combinator's Garry Tan is also listed, according to The Guardian. Thousands of signers describe themselves as Nvidia staff, though those affiliations are self-reported. But the numbers come with caveats. The Journal reported that some people said they never signed, even though their names appeared. And employment claims are not verified. The petition asks the board to bring Huang back while an independent investigation establishes the facts. It also asks the board to explain its evidence. Its backers argue he deserves due process. Two big shareholders sent their own letter. Brad Gerstner of Altimeter and Gavin Baker of Atreides urged the board to keep all options on the table, including the potential return of Jensen Huang. One caution. One analysis notes their combined stake is roughly 0.04%. Their influence comes from their profile, not their voting power. And here is the problem for Huang. Unlike some founder-CEOs, he does not hold a controlling stake. He cannot override the board. Nvidia's bylaws, according to one report, give the board alone the power to appoint a CEO. The board has not commented publicly on the petition. Now the pushback. On Sunday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called for a dramatic shakeup at Nvidia. He said the board should go outside the company for its next CEO. His words, per Reuters: as the board conducts a search, they need to go outside the company. One market commentary said this cooled hopes of a Huang return. And he set a deadline. Bessent said he wants answers within 10 days from the board and from outside counsel. The questions go beyond Huang. Bessent asked whether Nvidia ignored red flags that shell companies, many in Singapore, were diverting chips to China. He said other executives should be examined too. That matters. A founder returning is hard to square with a call for a dramatic shakeup. One commentary called the rally in comeback hopes fragile. But the White House picture is mixed. The New York Times reported that President Trump called Huang earlier and urged him to fight to keep his job. After the resignation, Trump said it's not yet clear whether Alice Huang was a spy. He also floated former Apple CEO Tim Cook for the top job, according to Yahoo Finance. Meanwhile, the board is not standing still. The FT reported that it approached Arm CEO Rene Haas as a potential successor. Nvidia has not publicly addressed Huang's possible return. And a spokesperson for Huang's side has not been cited in the reports we reviewed. Analysts are split, but mostly skeptical. Dan Ives of Yorkville said, quote, we see no chance Jensen comes back as CEO. He pointed to legal, board and Washington scrutiny. Lynx Equity's KC Rajkumar also sees almost no chance that Huang returns as CEO. But CNBC notes that some analysts see a path for Huang to return despite the controversy. One of them said a return would boost the stock. Prediction markets noticed. According to Fortune, about 40% of prediction-market bettors think Huang will return by next year. That earnings report is the next big test. Nvidia reports Tuesday, November 17, its first quarter since Huang left. Interim CEO Kress will lead the call. Analysts expect revenue of about $109.1 billion, up 91%, and earnings of $2.47 a share, according to CNBC. Huang answered most analyst questions on past calls, according to CNN. This time, Kress is likely to be pressed on China sales, neocloud deals and leadership. The stock fell about 2.7% premarket on Monday, near $158, after the FT report. Options, per one analysis, price a move of about 8% after results. Per another report, Nvidia stock fell 3% Monday as investors also weighed new US restrictions on overseas access to advanced AI chips. The stock has also fallen six straight weeks. There is also the legal front. Three House committees sent Huang a joint letter summoning him for a closed-door interview on December 1, according to CNN. And the New York Times reported that Huang hired the law firm Paul Weiss. Nvidia's board, meanwhile, has said its investigation continues. So what does it all mean? The petition, the war room and Musk's backing show real momentum on one side. The board, Bessent and many analysts are on the other. One report notes the decision rests with the board, and it has given no sign of reversing course. Nobody outside the room knows what the directors will do. Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993.
